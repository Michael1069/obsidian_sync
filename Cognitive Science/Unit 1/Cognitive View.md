## **Definition**

The **Cognitive View** explains the human mind as an **information-processing system**.

- It studies how humans **perceive, think, learn, remember, reason, and solve problems**.
    
- It assumes that the mind receives information, processes it, stores it, and produces appropriate responses.
    
- It compares the **brain to hardware** and the **mind to software**, where thinking is viewed as computation.
    

---

# **1. Mind as an Information Processing System**

The cognitive view considers the mind similar to a computer that processes information.

**Information Processing Cycle:**

```
Input → Processing → Memory → Output
```

- Information is received through the sensory organs.
    
- The brain processes the information using mental operations.
    
- Important information is stored in memory.
    
- Stored knowledge is retrieved whenever required for reasoning, learning, and decision-making.
    

---

# **2. Mental Representation**

Mental representation refers to the internal way the mind stores and represents information about the external world. It allows humans to think about objects or events even when they are not physically present.

### Types of Mental Representation

### a) Symbolic (Digital) Representation

- Information is stored as symbols, words, or numbers.
    
- Similar to the way computers store data.
    

### b) Analog (Imagery) Representation

- Information is stored as mental images.
    
- Preserves visual and spatial relationships.
    

### c) Propositional Representation

- Information is stored as facts, rules, and relationships.
    
- Example: **"The cat is on the mat."**
    

---

# **3. Computational Theory of Mind (CTM)**

The Computational Theory of Mind states that thinking is a form of computation.

### Main Ideas

- **Brain = Hardware**
    
- **Mind = Software**
    
- Thinking is an information-processing process.
    
- Mental activities are performed using algorithms.
    
- The brain manipulates internal symbols according to logical rules to produce meaningful thought.
    

---

# **4. Mental Computation**

Mental computation refers to the operations performed on mental representations.

It includes:

- Perception
    
- Attention
    
- Memory
    
- Language processing
    
- Reasoning
    
- Problem solving
    
- Decision making
    

These computations transform sensory information into meaningful knowledge and intelligent actions.

---

# **5. Marr's Three Levels of Analysis**

David Marr proposed three levels to explain how any cognitive system works.

### a) Computational Level (What & Why)

- Identifies the problem being solved.
    
- Explains why the computation is necessary.
    

**Example:** Recognizing an object while walking.

---

### b) Algorithmic Level (How)

- Explains the rules and algorithms used.
    
- Describes how information is represented and processed.
    

**Example:** Detecting edges, shapes, and patterns.

---

### c) Implementational Level (Physical Level)

- Explains how the computation is physically carried out.
    
- Involves neurons, brain structures, and neural circuits.
    

---

# **6. Symbolic and Connectionist Approaches**

The cognitive view explains intelligence using two major approaches.

## a) Symbolic (Classical) Approach

- Mind functions like a digital computer.
    
- Uses symbols and logical rules.
    
- Performs step-by-step reasoning.
    

## b) Connectionist (Neural Network) Approach

- Inspired by biological neurons.
    
- Knowledge is distributed across interconnected neural networks.
    
- Forms the basis of modern Artificial Intelligence and Deep Learning.

---

# **Advantages**

- Explains human thinking and learning scientifically.
    
- Forms the foundation of Artificial Intelligence and Cognitive Science.
    
- Helps understand memory, reasoning, perception, and problem-solving.
    

---

# **Limitations**

- Treats the human mind too much like a computer.
    
- Cannot fully explain emotions and consciousness.
    
- Gives less importance to social and cultural influences on cognition.
